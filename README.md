PART 2: DESIGN (Answer ALL questions)
What overall style will best represent you (minimalist, creative, professional, etc.)?
Professional.
What color scheme will you use and why?
Grays and reds, which are the colors of many important things in my life, mainly high school and Scouts.
What fonts will you use for headings and body text?
Arial for both headings and body text.
How will your design reflect your personality or field?
The design will be clean, polished, and professional, with a red accent color that adds personality while keeping the focus on my work.
What layout will your homepage follow?
A top navigation bar, followed by a hero section with my name, title (“Aspiring Video Game Programmer”), and a short tagline. Below that, a brief intro, a featured projects preview, and two prominent buttons (“View Projects” and “Contact Me”). A simple footer will hold my GitHub link.
How will you organize project sections visually?
Projects will appear as a grid of cards. Each card has a thumbnail or screenshot, the project title, a one- or two-sentence description, the technologies used, and a link to the GitHub repository.
Will the site be mobile-friendly? How will you ensure responsiveness?
Yes. I will use flexible layouts (CSS Flexbox/Grid and media queries) and test on multiple screen sizes and devices, including browser developer tools and real phones.
What visual hierarchy will guide visitors?
Large headings first, then subheadings, then body text. The red accent color will draw attention to key elements like buttons and links, with generous spacing between sections so the most important content (my name, projects, contact) stands out.
How will consistency be maintained across pages?
Every page will share the same navigation bar, footer, color scheme, font, and heading styles, and text and photos will be the same size across pages.
How will accessibility be considered (contrast, font size, readability)?
Text will meet the standard 4.5:1 contrast ratio and use a readable font size (at least 16px for body text). Images will have descriptive alt text, and the layout will be easy to navigate by keyboard.
Will you use icons, images, or illustrations? Why?
Yes, mainly photos, to show interesting things I have done, such as Scouting, fencing, and project screenshots. Simple icons (like the GitHub logo) will support navigation.

None in particular.


PART 3: INTERACTIVITY (Answer ALL questions)
What interactive elements will your site include (navigation menus, buttons, forms)?
A navigation menu, buttons, and a contact form.
Will your site include a contact form? How will it work?
Yes. When a visitor submits the form, the site will check that all required fields (name, email, message) are filled in and that the email format is valid. It will then display a confirmation message if the form is complete or an error message identifying what needs to be fixed.
What JavaScript features will you implement?
Form validation and a mobile menu toggle.
How will users receive feedback from interactions?
Buttons and links will change color or style on hover and focus. The form will show a clear success message or specific error messages, and the mobile menu will visibly open and close when toggled.
How does interactivity improve the user experience?
The navigation menu and buttons make it quick to find projects and contact information. The mobile toggle keeps the menu usable on small screens, and form validation gives immediate feedback so visitors can fix mistakes and know their message was sent.
